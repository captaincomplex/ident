What it does

Everything is driven by your real duty schedule. Times are stored in UTC and shown in whichever zone you prefer.

Reads your roster

Point it at your crew calendar (.ics) once. It re-checks every 30 minutes, so a roster change appears on the wall by itself. You can also upload a roster PDF.

Knows where you are in the day

Day off, standby, next duty, reported, airborne, turnaround, heading home — the screen changes to suit, instead of showing the same thing all day.

Tells you when you're home

on-chocks → debrief → walk → commute

The bit your family actually asks about. Tunable, and it sharpens as the duty progresses.

Tracks the live flight

While you're airborne it can follow the real sector — actual times and ETA — so the landing and home estimates follow reality, not the schedule.

Multi-sector days

A four-sector day shows every destination, not just the first — NTE/BOD — with report time and sector count on a button press.

Your data stays yours

Your roster lives on your device and is never sent to us. There's no account to create and no cloud service holding your schedule.

What you'll need

Ident is software. You buy the parts yourself — no markup from us, and nothing proprietary — then follow the illustrated build guide.

Important: the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W only works on 2.4GHz Wi-Fi.

It cannot see 5GHz networks at all. If your router only broadcasts 5GHz, or your network hides both bands behind a single name and steers devices to 5GHz, the Zero 2 W will fail to connect and there is no software fix. Check before you buy — and if in doubt, choose one of the dual-band boards below.

Board2.4GHz5GHzNotes
Pi Zero 2 WYesNo Cheapest and smallest, and fine on a dual-band router that still broadcasts 2.4GHz. Needs a 40-pin header soldered unless you buy the pre-soldered “WH” version.
Pi 3 Model B+YesYes Dual-band and has Ethernet. Bulkier behind the frame, but a safe choice on awkward networks.
Pi 4 Model BYesYes Dual-band, Ethernet, plenty quick. Runs warmer and needs a USB-C supply.
Pi 5YesYes Overkill for this, but works. Needs a 5V/5A USB-C supply.
Zero 2 W + USB Wi-FiYesYes A dual-band USB adapter with Linux support, via a micro-USB OTG adapter. Cheapest 5GHz route, but the fiddliest.
Full parts list and the combinations to avoid: see the parts page, and the illustrated assembly guide (PDF).
Also on the list: a microSD card (16GB is ample), a power supply to suit your board, and a frame or stand. Any 40-pin Raspberry Pi works — the display sits straight on the header, no soldering needed if your Pi has one fitted.

Honest limitations

Things worth knowing before you spend money.

It's not a clock

Colour e-paper takes around 30 seconds to redraw, so Ident updates when something changes — not every second. If you want a live ticking display, this isn't it.

Roster support varies

Built and tested against easyJet eCrew calendar feeds. Other airlines' formats may need work — ask us before buying if you fly elsewhere.

You build it

No soldering if you buy the right parts, but you will flash an SD card and follow a guide. Budget an unhurried hour.
